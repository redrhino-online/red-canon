Five steps

  1. Plan in advance.
  2. Craft the pitch.
  3. Frame the funnel.
  4. Fill the follow up.
  5. Measure and scale.

Step 1: Plan

Know what your audience must think, feel, and do.

  • Who is this for?
  • What do they want?
  • What stops them?

Step 2: Craft the pitch

Six parts, in order. Each part gets slides.

Offer

Frame: five minutes

  • A clear promise.
  • Why this is different.
  • Who it is for.
  • A reason to stay.

Teach: most of the time

Show what to do, not every how.

Teach three steps, three times, one per stage.

Context first. Detail later.

The teach pattern

1Promise a result.
2Name three obstacles.
3Show the steps.
4Give proof.
5Ask a small yes.

Shift: five minutes

Move from teaching to selling, calmly.

I showed you what. If you want help with how, let me show you.

Offer: ten minutes

  • Who it is for, and who it is not.
  • A short product tour.
  • What it does, and why it matters.

Stack the value

Show real value. Not hype numbers.

  • The normal price.
  • The event price.
  • A reason to act now.

Show and close

  • Welcome new clients by name.
  • Show the first quick win.
  • Answer the top doubts.
  • Repeat the offer and the risk reversal.

Make the offer safe

Take the risk off the buyer.

If it is not right, they get their money back.

Email that gets opened

  • A short subject.
  • One clear idea.
  • One clear link.

Retarget by choice

Show ads to people based on what they did.

  • Signed up but did not show.
  • Showed but did not buy.

The automated webinar

Record it once. Run it many times.

  • Prove it live first.
  • Run it live ten times well.
  • Then automate it.

Keep it lively

Bland slides lose the room.

  • Big images. Short text.
  • Energy and pace.
  • Ask questions often.

Common mistakes

  • Teaching one topic too deep.
  • A long sales pitch with little teaching.
  • Fake scarcity.
  • No follow up for no-shows.

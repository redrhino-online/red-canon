Plan a recurring campaign around a seasonal theme or a theme from the client's main product or service.

The full cycle

Fit and goal
New theme
Plan and invite
Deliver and follow up
Measure and learn

Each run ends with a theme check for the next run.

1 · Check fit and goal

Lead: coach with client. Input: portfolio, past buyers, warm leads (people who have shown interest but have not bought), team capacity, and the client's goal.

  • Confirm there is a useful offer and a reason to contact these groups.
  • Agree what this run should test and how to judge it.
  • Check that the team can prepare, host, and follow up.

Gate: if fit, goal, or capacity is unclear, gather facts or pause. Output: agreed goal and owner list.

2 · Inspect past runs

Lead: campaign owner. Review past themes, dates or seasons used, audience response, and notes from the team.

  • Write down what was offered and what each run taught you.
  • Separate known results from guesses.
  • List themes that have not been used.

If records are missing, ask the client and mark gaps. Do not treat a guess as a past result.

3 · Choose a distinct theme

Lead: client with coach. Choose one seasonal idea or one part of the client's main product or service.

  • Match it to the agreed goal and audience needs.
  • Compare it with every past run.
  • Reject a repeat of the same theme. Record why this one is distinct.

Gate: client approves the theme and reason. If no strong theme fits, research more or delay the run.

4 · Define the audience

Lead: client and campaign owner. Use consented, current contact records.

First: past buyers
Also: warm leads
  • Note each group's needs, past actions, and fit for this theme.
  • Plan a relevant message for each group.
  • Remove people who should not receive the invitation.

Output: checked segments and a reason to invite each one.

5 · Shape the event and offer

Lead: client with coach. Choose an event type and a clear offer that fit the theme.

  • Set the event purpose, format, promise, owner, and attendee next step.
  • Define who the offer is for, what it includes, and how to respond.
  • Check that the promise is useful and supported. Keep terms clear.

Gate: client approves event and offer. If they do not fit the audience or capacity, reshape them before building.

6 · Prepare the campaign

Lead: campaign owner. Set the schedule with the client; no one schedule fits every run.

  • Write the invitation, reminders, event messages, and follow-up.
  • Choose channels with the client and build the needed pages and assets.
  • Assign each task and due point to an owner.

Output: approved messages, schedule, assets, and event plan.

7 · Check readiness

Lead: producer; client signs off. Test the full path before inviting people.

  • Check links, sign-up, audience lists, message timing, and reply handling.
  • Check event access, materials, roles, and the offer next step.
  • Confirm owners know what to do if a tool or event step fails.

Gate: all critical items pass. If one fails, fix and test again; pause invites if people could be misled or blocked.

8 · Invite and remind

Lead: campaign owner. Invite past buyers first and include warm leads as planned.

  • Send each group the message that fits its history and needs.
  • Send reminders on the approved schedule.
  • Watch sign-ups, replies, and delivery problems. Help people who need access.

If a message or link fails, stop the affected send, correct it, and tell people what changed.

9 · Deliver the event

Lead: event host; producer supports. Use the agreed run of show.

  • Welcome people and set the purpose.
  • Teach or guide the event toward its promised result.
  • Present the offer and next step clearly; answer questions.

If access or delivery breaks, use the agreed backup, inform attendees, and record who needs help.

10 · Follow up

Lead: campaign owner. Use the plan approved for each audience group.

  • Send useful next steps and event materials where promised.
  • Answer questions and make the offer path clear.
  • Respect contact choices and stop messages when required.

Output: follow-up sent, open questions assigned, and audience actions recorded.

11 · Measure this test

Lead: client and campaign owner. Compare results with the agreed goal and starting result (the result before this campaign).

  • Review reach, sign-ups, attendance, replies, offer actions, and effort as relevant.
  • Compare past buyers and warm leads where records allow.
  • Ask whether repeat purchase or interest in the new theme showed up.

These are hypotheses, not guarantees. Choose measures with the client; do not force universal targets.

12 · Record learning

Lead: campaign owner with client. Save the goal, theme, segments, actions, results, and limits in the run record.

  • Note what worked, what did not, and what you cannot yet tell.
  • Keep facts separate from ideas for next time.
  • Choose a distinct future theme and check it against all past runs.

End result: a reviewed run, clear learning, and a non-repeating theme candidate. If none is ready, record the research needed.

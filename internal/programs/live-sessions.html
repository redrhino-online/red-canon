Live session delivery map

Owner: facilitator. Reviewer checks the offer; note taker records decisions. Inputs: learner question, current offer, numbers, session goal, and time limit.

  1. Ask the learner for the goal and the facts that matter.
  2. Choose one session outcome and agree how to tell it is complete.
  3. Review the one-page offer, customer, result, steps, and next action.
  4. Draft five hooks and test them against the customer language.
  5. Check price against result and delivery; do not discount without changing scope.
  6. Choose a funnel that fits price and team capacity.
  7. Use call gates for intent, urgency, and confidence; stop or revisit when a gate fails.
  8. Give a written action plan with an owner and due date for each task.
  9. At the next session, check completion and numbers, then choose the next bottleneck.

Gate: do not prescribe from guesses; ask for missing facts or set a task to collect them. If the session runs out, record what is unfinished and book the next decision. End result: a clear offer or funnel decision and owned next actions.

Live session · prepare and diagnose

Start with a booked session, a time limit, a learner goal, the current offer, customer facts, and any useful numbers. The facilitator leads; a note taker captures facts, choices, owners, and dates.

  1. Ask what the learner wants to change and what is happening now. Gather lead, booking, sale, price, and delivery facts where they matter. Separate known facts from guesses. If key facts are missing, agree who will collect them.
  2. Set one outcome for this meeting and how both sides will know it is done. Check that it fits the time. Output: a short session aim and baseline.
  3. Review the one-page offer: who it serves, the result, the method, proof, price, and next action. Ask what buyers have tried and where they get stuck before prescribing a change.
  4. Write five distinct hooks from the buyer’s own language. Compare each with the offer promise and proof. Keep only hooks that speak to the same person and result; revise vague or unsupported claims.
  5. Check price against the result, delivery cost, and capacity. Choose a simple path to the next step that fits the price and sales process. Do not use a discount to hide a poor fit; change scope or value openly.

Gate and recovery: do not move from questions to advice until the goal and facts are clear. If they are not, assign a fact-finding task and set a return date. Handoff: the note taker shares the offer draft, hook choices, baseline, and open questions with the learner after the session.

Live session · decide and follow through

  1. For a sales discussion, check intent, urgency, and confidence in order. Ask a clear question at each point and listen to the answer. If a check fails, go back to the concern or stop; do not push past it.
  2. Turn the session into a short action plan. Each task needs one owner, a due date, and a visible result. Put the chosen offer or funnel decision first, then the smallest useful test.
  3. At the next meeting, review completed tasks and compare the numbers with the starting point. If the result did not move, locate the first broken step and ask what changed before choosing a new action.
  4. Use a recorded teaching session again only when it helps answer a repeated question. Mark useful sections for clips or notes, check that each piece has context and one next action, and send the assets to the content owner.
  5. Gate: end only when the learner can state the decision, next action, owner, and date. If time runs out, name what is unfinished and book the next decision instead of implying it is complete.

Output and handoff: learner receives the decision, action list, baseline, due dates, and any useful recording; facilitator carries open items into the next session. Measurable result: each session records its agreed outcome, task completion, and change in the selected business measure.

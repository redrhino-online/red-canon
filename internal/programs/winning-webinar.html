Frame: five minutes

  • A clear promise.
  • Why this is different.
  • Who it is for.
  • A reason to stay.

Teach: most of the time

Show what to do, not every how.

Teach three steps, three times, one per stage.

Context first. Detail later.

The teach pattern

1Promise a result.
2Name three obstacles.
3Show the steps.
4Give proof.
5Ask a small yes.

Shift: five minutes

Move from teaching to selling, calmly.

I showed you what. If you want help with how, let me show you.

Offer: ten minutes

  • Who it is for, and who it is not.
  • A short product tour.
  • What it does, and why it matters.

Stack the value

Show real value. Not hype numbers.

  • The normal price.
  • The event price.
  • A reason to act now.

Show and close

  • Welcome new clients by name.
  • Show the first quick win.
  • Answer the top doubts.
  • Repeat the offer and the risk reversal.

Make the offer safe

Webinar delivery map

Owner: presenter. Producer checks links and timing; sales owner handles replies. Inputs: proven offer, audience, proof, signup page, email tool, and live date.

  1. Set the audience, one promise, offer, price, and attendance goal.
  2. Check the promise with likely attendees before writing slides.
  3. Draft the webinar in order: frame, teach, shift, offer, show, close.
  4. Build signup, confirmation, reminders, live room, checkout, and replay.
  5. Send invitation and reminder messages; test each link and time zone.
  6. Rehearse with a timer and test sound, screen, slides, and checkout.
  7. Deliver the teaching, answer questions, make a clear offer, and record attendance.
  8. Send replay and helpful answers to attendees and no-shows.
  9. Compare signups, attendance, sales, refunds, and spend; fix one weak stage.

Gate: run a full test registration and purchase before inviting the list. If the live room or checkout fails, use the backup link, tell registrants, and send the replay. Use sample rates only as planning targets. End result: a delivered webinar and a measured follow-up path.

Webinar build · before you invite

Start with a proven offer, a defined audience, a clear next action, proof, a signup page, email access, and a date. The presenter owns the story; a producer owns links and timing.

  1. Choose one buyer group, one urgent problem, one measurable promise, and the action you want after the webinar. Check that your offer can deliver the promise. Output: one-page webinar brief.
  2. Outline three teaching parts that fit the whole solution. For each part, state a result, the main obstacles, the steps, proof, and a small check that the audience agrees. Avoid solving only one small piece without showing the full path.
  3. Build the talk in order: opening promise and reason to stay; three useful teaching parts; a calm move from teaching to help; who the offer fits; what is included and why it matters; price, terms, questions, and next step. Use real proof and a fair deadline only when one exists.
  4. Build signup, confirmation, reminders, live room, checkout or booking, replay, and follow-up. Put the reason to attend on the confirmation page. Mark each success page as a measured goal.
  5. Gate: register, click every link, test sound and screen share, and complete a test checkout or booking. If any step fails, fix it before invitations go out. Send a backup access path to the producer.

Handoff: the brief and run sheet go to the presenter; tested links and attendee list go to the producer. Recovery: if a tool fails on event day, tell registrants where to join, use the backup room, and send the recording and working next step afterward.

Webinar run · learn · repeat

  1. Send the invite, useful reminders, and a final-time note. Track registrations by source. The confirmation page and reminders should make the value and time clear.
  2. Present to the promised time. Teach the full map, invite questions, make the offer plainly, and record attendance, questions, clicks, and purchases. The producer flags tech issues and captures unanswered questions.
  3. Send attendees the next step and answers; send no-shows a replay and a clear way to continue. Stop reminders for people who already bought or booked. Use a separate follow-up for each group.
  4. Review the chain: spend per registration, registration-to-attendance rate, attendance-to-sale rate, revenue, refunds, and value per buyer. Find the first weak step before changing anything else.
  5. Gate: repeat live until the content, show-up path, and offer work reliably. If attendance is weak, improve confirmation and reminders; if people attend but do not act, review the promise, teaching, offer fit, and unanswered doubts. Change one main item and compare the next run.

Output and handoff: a recorded webinar, segmented follow-up list, run report, and one named improvement go to the next event owner. Measurable result: a completed live run with all funnel rates and sales recorded; automate only after the live version has repeated results.

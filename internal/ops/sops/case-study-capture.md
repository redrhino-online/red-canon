# Case study capture SOP

This procedure turns a client result into a story. It covers confirm the
result, get permission, write, approve, and publish.

## Trigger

A client hits a goal or gets a clear win. The delivery lead starts this
procedure within one week.

## Roles

- Delivery lead: confirms the result and asks for permission.
- Writer: writes the story.
- Client: reviews the story and approves it.

## Procedure

1. Confirm the result. Check the numbers against the goals set at kickoff.
2. Gather the facts. Note the start point, the change, and the end point.
3. Get permission. Ask the client in writing if we can share the story.
4. Set the terms. Agree on what we can name and what we must hide.
5. Write the story. Use the client's words. Keep it short and clear.
6. Add the proof. Put in the numbers and one short quote.
7. Send it for approval. Give the client time to read and change it.
8. Fix the story. Make the changes the client asks for.
9. Publish the story. Put it where the plan says. Tell the client.
10. Save the file. Put the final story and the permission note in the folder.

Never publish a story without written permission.

flowchart LR
    A[Confirm] --> B[Permission] --> C[Write] --> D[Publish]

## Decision points

- If the result is not clear, wait. Do not write a story on a guess.
- If the client says no, stop. Do not publish anything.
- If the client wants to stay private, hide the name and the details.
- If the numbers are weak, use the story of the change, not the size.

## Escalation

Tell the delivery lead when a client will not approve a story or when the facts
do not match. If the client is unhappy, tell the operations owner.

## Records

Save the permission note, the final story, and the proof in the client folder.
Keep the publish date and the place with the story.
